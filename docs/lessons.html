Lessons

What this project and earlier ones taught. Newest first.

From this project

  • Measure where the learner fails before changing the lessons. At gen 382 the champion landed ~90% past the full boulder in calm air but only 73% in full wind with no boulder: the wind was the bottleneck, so restarting with a small rock and no wind would have re-taught a known skill.
  • Pace a curriculum by the learner's success, not by a clock: in the wind stage the champion landed 97–100% while the ramp waited; paced ramps from gen 230 passed the stage at gen 301.
  • Test that a resumed run uses the live settings. The species set came back from its checkpoint with an old threshold (46.8) and ignored every change for 140 generations, until species fell from 13 to 3.
  • Choose a NEAT threshold by simulating on the saved population (0.3 → 79 species, 0.4 → 13), not with a feedback controller that can run away.
  • Species protect new structure: too few and new neurons die before they become useful, too many and each group is too small (105 species × 3 minimum members > 300 genomes stopped the run).
  • Speed up the biggest part and the next part becomes the wall: after the C++ simulator, neat-python's crossover took a third of each generation.
  • A fast simulator is only allowed once it is bit-identical to the reference (3,000 of 3,000 flights). Even the reference is not repeatable across history: Box2D reuses ids of deleted bodies.
  • The physics solver is the speed floor (180 velocity + 60 position iterations per step): 5.3×, not the planned 10×. Hyperthreads add little: 8 workers are only 17% faster than 4 on 4 cores.
  • 200-flight checks swing widely (76% at gen 402, 52% at gen 442), and the best of many looks better than it is (winner's curse): choose on 1,000 fresh flights.

From the last two projects

  • Learning signal decides speed: one number per flight needs many flights, shared seeds and fast code.
  • Start clean: a copied pilot is a cage for RL and gets wrecked by NEAT's mutations.
  • Measure the network size you need before evolving it.
  • Hard cases are the curriculum for the last few percent (PPO: 92.7% → ~96%).
  • A stage pass on 400 flights is luck after enough tries; choose champions on thousands of fresh flights.
  • Check that the world is fair, and prove it with a test: v2 let the boulder sit 1.73 m above the pad, lower than the lander is tall.
  • Sensing beats network size: the clean wind sensor (0.97 correlation with the true wind) lifted the hand-written pilot from 75% to 86%.
  • Pay for landings, not for the game score (score 182 → 220 while landings fell).
  • Profile before rewriting; keep the reference simulator as the judge.
  • Determinism, resume and git hygiene bugs cost the most time: test them first. Never git pull --rebase --autostash in a publisher.
